# Heads-up preflop-to-flop research handoff

`PreflopFlopTransition` connects a completed **two-player, non-all-in** branch of the six-seat preflop betting tree to the existing three-street flop game. For example, after UTG/HJ/CO fold, BTN opens to 3bb, SB folds and BB calls, the flop game starts with a 6.5bb pot and 97bb behind. BB acts first on the flop. The folded small blind's 0.5bb remains in the pot.

The caller supplies each surviving seat's prior exact-combo range and a likelihood in `[0, 1]` for the **observed preflop action history** for every combo. It also names the exact actions being conditioned; these must match that seat's decisions in the completed betting history. The handoff multiplies prior weight by that likelihood, removes zero-weight combos, rejects impossible joint deals and then removes hands blocked by the public flop. These likelihoods are explicit inputs: PokerLab does **not** yet have a solved six-seat opening/calling policy that can produce them. The projection retains the public history and both source labels so synthetic research inputs remain identifiable.

For a particular unordered flop, the handoff computes its probability from the conditioned joint hand distribution. With two known two-card hands, each compatible flop has probability `1 / C(48, 3)`; the result is weighted by the fraction of legal joint deals compatible with that flop. The projected `FlopTurnRiverSpot` uses the full 49-card public turn candidate deck, with the existing game removing private-card blockers at each deal. The projected spot carries the preflop pot, equal remaining stacks and caller-selected flop/turn/river bet sizes. Its content hash includes the resulting ranges and game sizes.

This is a **research bridge**, not a published GTO solution. It currently requires equal live commitments and stacks, no rake and exactly two survivors. It does not infer action likelihoods, model the card-correlated fold ranges of the other four seats, solve across every flop, or validate the chosen postflop bet abstraction. The current three-street solver is expensive even for small ranges, so constructing a spot is deliberately separate from solving it. The next step is to derive and audit preflop action policies, sample representative flops, and measure computation and abstraction error before building a trainer pack.

Run `mvn -pl solver -am test` for the transition, blocker, action-order, accounting and saved-pack regressions.
