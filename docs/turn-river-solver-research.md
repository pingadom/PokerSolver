# Turn-to-river chance-node research

The solver now has a bounded two-street heads-up game: BB and BTN make turn decisions, a legal river card is dealt publicly, and both make river decisions before showdown. An opt-in local research drill uses a saved solution from this game. It does not provide a continuation value for a preflop call.

The reproducible [validation fixture](../solver/src/main/java/com/pokerlab/solver/TurnRiverValidationSpot.java) starts with board `2c 3d 4h 8s`, a 20bb pot, 80bb remaining per player, and one 10bb bet size on each street. BB's exact combos are `Ah As` and `6s 7s`; BTN's are `Kc Kd` and `Qc Qd`, all equally weighted. These ranges are synthetic. The model has no rake, raises, or all-in sizing, and assumes every listed combo has reached the turn.

On each street BB can check or bet. After a check BTN can check or bet. A bet faces call or fold. A turn fold ends the hand. After a turn check-check or bet-call, the game deals one of the 44 cards not in the four-card board or either player's hole cards. The river card is visible to both players; the opponent's hole cards remain hidden. Showdown evaluates all seven cards exactly. The river information set includes the acting player's exact combo, the complete turn betting history, the river card and current river history. The turn information set excludes the future river card. Thus the solver cannot condition a turn action on a card it has not seen.

Chip utility is centered on half the starting pot. A turn fold gives the winner +10bb and loser −10bb. After a called turn bet, river showdown without another called bet gives ±20bb; after a called river bet it gives ±30bb. River folds use half the pot then in play. This is a zero-sum no-rake model. Centering does not change optimal actions or the best-response gap.

The [general information-set best-response evaluator](../solver/src/main/java/com/pokerlab/solver/HeadsUpBestResponse.java) holds one player's strategy fixed, weights hidden states by chance and that opponent's action probabilities, and selects one pure response per information set. It handles chance nodes after player actions. It reports each player's deviation bound and their difference. Tests compare its answers with the independently implemented preflop and river best-response evaluators, as well as the known Kuhn benchmark. The evaluator assumes the `CfrGame` contract of perfect recall; it is not a proof that an arbitrary new game implements that contract correctly.

The [versioned validation pack](../solver/src/test/resources/turn-river-validation-pack.json) saves all 1,112 exact-combo strategies, a hash of the rules and weighted ranges, the solver iteration count and the recomputed best-response gap. Loading checks every reachable information set, legal action and probability before accepting the pack. A full-content hash identifies links to the specific saved solution. The pack is generated offline; API requests never launch CFR.

From the repository root, after `mvn -q -pl solver -am test-compile`, run `java -cp 'solver/target/classes;engine/target/classes' com.pokerlab.solver.BenchmarkTurnRiver 3000` (PowerShell syntax) to reproduce the offline measurement. On this fixture, 3,000 alternating CFR+ iterations created **1,112 information sets**, a profile value of **+5.492343bb for BB**, and a **0.000549bb best-response gap**. The same fixture had a 0.055626bb gap at 100 iterations and 0.001574bb at 1,000 iterations. These are exact-game strategy gaps, with no board sampling error; they measure only this small, prescribed game.

`TurnRiverDecisionEvaluator` grades either street. At a river decision it excludes private deals blocked by the public river, weights the remaining opponent combos by their observed turn and river actions, and holds the hero's own earlier actions fixed. A turn action EV averages all legal future river cards and saved later-street actions. This is one-action EV loss in the saved model, not a claim about a full-hand strategy.

The API is disabled by default. For local research set `TRAINER_TURN_RIVER_RESEARCH_ENABLED=true` and `TRAINER_TURN_RIVER_RESEARCH_PACK_PATH` to the absolute path of the saved pack, or use the trainer Compose overlay. `GET /api/v1/trainer/research/turn-river` returns public metadata; `GET /api/v1/trainer/research/turn-river/questions/{seed}` returns a deterministic turn or river question with no EVs or opponent cards; `POST /api/v1/trainer/research/turn-river/grade` accepts `{"seed":"42","packHash":"…","action":"k"}` with one of that question's legal action codes. Server-side grading rejects stale pack hashes, illegal actions and submitted EV fields. The local website route `#turn-river` shows the board and prior public actions, keeps the river hidden on turn questions, and reveals EV feedback only after an answer. The trainer samples the two streets equally; its available decisions are still drawn from a tiny synthetic game.

The question's pot includes any outstanding bet already made on the current street; its remaining stack describes the acting player before choosing. These values are display context. The action EVs use the centered starting-pot convention above.

Before turning this into a reviewed lesson, the ranges must be tied to explicit earlier-street actions, alternative bet sizes and rake must be modeled, and the approximate strategy must be checked against game-abstraction error. A coherent full-hand trainer also needs earlier-street transitions that preserve these public histories and posterior ranges.
