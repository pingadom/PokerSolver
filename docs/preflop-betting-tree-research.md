# Bounded six-seat preflop betting rules

`SixMaxPreflopBetting` is the next solver foundation after the forced-shove fixtures. It advances all six seats in UTG, HJ, CO, BTN, SB, BB order, starting from posted 0.5bb/1bb blinds. A configured menu limits raise-to amounts; the reference 100bb game offers 3, 10, 22, 40 and 100bb. The engine enforces turn order, total commitment, minimum full raises, no action after folding, and the big blind's option to check when no one raises. A raise reopens action for every other live seat. All chip amounts use integer millionths of a big blind internally.

Each immutable state exposes committed chips, live seats, pot, amount to call, legal actions and public history. It distinguishes three outcomes:

| Outcome | What the model knows |
| --- | --- |
| Everyone else folds | The remaining seat wins the pot, including folded blind money; per-seat terminal chip payoffs need no hidden cards. |
| All live seats commit 100bb | Given six distinct physical hole-card combinations, a showdown oracle can now value every surviving seat and settle the pot. |
| Two or more live seats finish below 100bb | The state is `POSTFLOP_CONTINUATION_REQUIRED`. It has no invented terminal EV. |

The existing UTG-versus-BTN five-bet spot now replays its action history through these rules at construction. It still has the same content hash, saved solution and restricted shove/fold continuation. The stronger validation rejects histories that the earlier contribution-only check could accept, such as swapping the HJ and CO actions or making a below-minimum re-raise. The reference history leaves UTG facing an 18bb call in a 63.5bb pot. If UTG shoves and BTN folds, UTG's net win is 41.5bb; if BTN calls, the final pot is 201.5bb. Those amounts match [ADR-004](decisions/ADR-004-preflop-validation-game.md).

`SixMaxPreflopTerminalPayoff` now connects those public betting states to `AllInSidePots`. It returns the utility of **each** seat, rake paid, active-seat mask and maximum payoff sampling error. A fold win can be settled from public history alone. For an all-in showdown, the caller supplies all six physical hole-card combinations in seat order, including folded hands, so removed cards cannot reappear on the board. The same oracle interface supports exact board enumeration or a declared sampled estimate. A configurable rake rule applies only when explicitly supplied; the default no-rake rule preserves earlier fixtures. A state that needs flop continuation is rejected instead of receiving a made-up showdown EV.

## Final-decision conditional EV

`SixMaxFinalDecisionEv.evaluate` values the last player's fold and call when both branches finish preflop and the call leads to an all-in showdown. It accepts the betting game and decision, the acting player's shown physical combo, six **action-conditioned** weighted ranges in UTG-to-BB order, a declared rake rule, and a showdown oracle. It enumerates legal joint deals, including cards held by players who already folded. The shown hand is fixed; opponent weights are multiplied and renormalized *after* removing card collisions. Log weights avoid overflow from large range weights. The fold payoff is the negative of chips already committed by the acting player; the call payoff averages full six-seat terminal settlements. The result gives both chip EVs, their difference, the number of legal deals, and a conservative bound from showdown-payoff sampling errors. That bound excludes uncertainty in the ranges or their action likelihoods.

The exact path is deliberately capped at **64 legal joint deals** before any showdown work. Larger ranges fail explicitly rather than silently sampling or reporting an apparently exact result. An impossible shown hand, duplicate range combos, incomplete seat ranges, an earlier decision with actions still pending, and a call that needs postflop play also fail. This is a **conditional response calculation**, not a six-player equilibrium strategy: the caller must supply defensible per-seat ranges for the observed betting history. The engine does not infer those ranges from preflop actions or certify them for trainer content.

### Connecting an existing solved response policy

`SixMaxForcedShovePolicyBridge` now supplies those action-conditioned ranges for **one narrow source game**: a 100bb UTG shove, followed by HJ, CO, BTN and SB call/fold decisions, with BB last. It reads the original physical-combo ranges and the solved call/fold frequencies in `MultiwayPreflopCallGame`. For an observed public history, each preceding player's combo weight is multiplied by that player's probability of taking the observed action at the matching public prefix. The six-seat evaluator then removes overlapping physical cards and renormalizes the legal joint deals. Folded players' cards remain blockers. The bridge reuses the source game's showdown oracle and rake rule, so it cannot silently grade one strategy against a different payoff model.

This is a constrained way to carry **policy-derived likelihoods** into the newer betting engine; it does not make the synthetic source ranges realistic. The bridge rejects changed stacks, blind commitments, seat order, dead money, missing strategy keys, impossible observed actions and histories outside this forced-shove branch. In a two-deal analytic control, an HJ call changes the relative weights of two HJ combos from 1:3 to 0.8:0.6. A fold changes them to 0.2:2.4. The corresponding BB call EVs differ, and each matches an independent conditional average of the source game's terminal utilities. Additional tests solve the source game with CFR+ and verify twelve seeded BB trainer questions against the bridge under both no-rake and capped-rake rules. Neither comparison establishes equilibrium quality beyond this restricted game.

`SixMaxForcedShoveResponderEv` now carries that comparison to **all five responding seats**. For a shown physical hand and any observed prefix of HJ-to-SB actions, it weights each legal six-hand deal by the source chance probability and the earlier players' policy likelihoods. It holds the acting player's fold or call fixed, then follows the solved policy for every later responder through `SixMaxPreflopBetting`. Every terminal branch is settled by `SixMaxPreflopTerminalPayoff` with the source game's oracle and rake rule. The returned fold/call chip EV and call payoff-sampling-error bound are conditional on the observed prefix and hand; range and policy-model error are outside that bound. At most 64 reachable physical deals are accepted per evaluation, before payoff work begins.

Cross-checks compare twelve seeded questions at **each** of the five responder seats against the existing multiway trainer, both without rake and with 5% capped rake. A separate check matches the earlier BB range-based evaluator on three public histories, and a sampled-oracle control verifies error propagation through later policy decisions. This strengthens the accounting and policy-conditioning bridge, but the strategy still comes from the restricted, synthetic forced-shove game. It does not value open/raise branches, non-all-in calls, or postflop continuation.

## Complete bounded preflop research game

`SixMaxPreflopCheckdownGame` is the first `MultiPlayerCfrGame` backed directly by the six-seat betting rules from the initial blind posts. It has physical private-card chance, each seat's fold/call/check options, a configured raise-to menu, and literal chip payoffs. It prebuilds the public action tree and terminal payoff table before solving. Information sets contain the acting player's own combo and the complete public action history; opponent cards never appear in the player's information set. Research fixtures exercise both a single 100bb shove target and a 3bb/100bb raise menu. It accepts at most **64 legal joint deals** and **20,000 public states**, with a further **160,000 deal × public-state** cap before payoff work. The broader raise menu therefore allows fewer deals than the shove-only tree. Larger games fail explicitly rather than silently truncating.

The explicit closure rule is **mandatory checkdown after any non-all-in preflop round**: all live players see a board with no later betting. The same showdown oracle values their physical hands, including folded-card blockers, and `AllInSidePots` divides the committed pot with the declared rake rule. Called all-ins use `SixMaxPreflopTerminalPayoff`; uncontested fold wins need no board. This is a complete, internally consistent *research game* that lets CFR+ visit opens, calls, checks, raises, folds and showdowns from UTG onward. Mandatory checkdown is a game rule for validation, **not** a claimed approximation to real postflop strategy. A policy solved here is not a general six-max GTO chart or approved trainer content.

Tests check all three terminal types, a 3bb open and three-way call, every terminal's chip conservation under capped rake, range-weighted chance probabilities, hidden-card information-set separation, an exact physical-board checkdown, and a small CFR+ solve. This establishes the mechanics needed for a later connected continuation game. The next quality gates are a credible postflop action model, position-specific range provenance, larger-game computation, and stable low deviation across independent research inputs.

### Exact deviation audit inside the bounded game

`MultiPlayerInformationSetBestResponse` now computes each player's **exact pure best response against a fixed six-seat profile within the finite supplied game**. It groups states by the responding player's information set, weights hidden deals and earlier opponent actions by their chance/opponent reach, then chooses actions from the deepest information sets backward. This handles a player acting more than once after raises. The report includes profile utility, best-response utility, per-seat unilateral gain, their sum (`NashConv`), and the chosen actions at every information set. The calculation uses the game's precomputed payoff table; if that table came from sampled showdown estimates, its sampling error remains a separate limit.

Controls recover a known two-decision response without revealing hidden chance, match the older forced-shove best-response checker on an independent game, and verify nonnegative gains for both no-rake and raked six-seat checkdown games. The metric is exact **for this checkdown model and its input ranges**. A small `NashConv` would not certify a realistic 6-max cash strategy, because the mandatory-checkdown rule, restricted raise menu and synthetic ranges remain part of the game definition. Multi-player CFR+ is also not guaranteed to drive this metric monotonically downward; the audit measures a saved profile rather than assuming convergence.

A [reproducible six-seat convergence audit](sixmax-preflop-convergence-audit.md) now re-solves two physical-card fixtures across multiple CFR+ budgets, both with and without capped rake. It records exact-board payoff error, per-seat deviation, and profile chip conservation, including a 3bb/open-shove tree. The result is a quality gate for the bounded research game, not trainer-ready strategy data.

### Validation-only full-seat trainer backend

`SixMaxPreflopResearchTrainer` can now draw a seeded physical deal, follow the saved six-seat policy through the preflop betting tree, and present one reached decision from any seat. A question exposes only the acting seat's own hand, prior public actions, pot, amount to call, legal actions, stack/blind values, the game's chance-model provenance, and its deviation and payoff-error measurements. It carries `VALIDATION_ONLY`; it does not disclose hidden opponent hands or answer EVs.

For grading, the trainer replays the public history over **all compatible hidden joint deals**. Each deal is weighted by its chance probability and the likelihood of every observed prior action under the fixed policy. It then evaluates each legal action while all later decisions follow that same policy. Feedback reports conditional action EVs, the chosen action's EV loss against the best one-step action, and the saved policy's action frequencies. The submitted question must match the one regenerated from its seed, so altered cards or histories cannot change the conditioning. An analytic two-deal control checks fold/call/shove EVs of 0bb, 0.5bb and 50bb when a hidden BTN hand has 1:3 prior weight. Another control makes BTN more likely to call with one hand than the other; after a public BTN call, BB's check EV uses the resulting 3/7 posterior probability of BTN holding 55 rather than its 3/4 prior. Further checks cover reproducible sampling across seats and rejection of tampered or illegal submissions.

Feedback also exposes `actionPayoffStandardErrorBb`, a conditional estimated payoff-SE envelope for each legal action. It uses the same hidden-deal posterior as the action EV. At each continuation it sums probability-weighted terminal SE envelopes rather than assuming leaves sampled from shared boards are independent. A folded hero's payoff has zero sampling error; active heroes conservatively use the terminal's maximum per-seat SE. Synthetic controls give a root fold/call/shove envelope of 0bb/0.06bb/6bb and verify the 4/7 versus 3/7 posterior after a BTN call. These envelopes exclude policy uncertainty, empirical chance-support error and checkdown-model error, and are not confidence intervals or a bound on the selected maximum action's EV loss.

This backend is a bridge to a broader trainer, not a published six-max curriculum. It grades decisions in the same tiny mandatory-checkdown game as the convergence audit; realistic range provenance, postflop continuation, and scale remain open gates before serving these questions as real strategy advice.

The [saved full-round pack and session API](sixmax-full-round-pack-trainer.md) now persists this bounded tree, its complete subset payoffs and policy. A committed exact-board fixture supports ten-decision sessions through an opt-in backend API and the local trainer Compose overlay. Reload checks strategy coverage and measured NashConv; question, grade and review requests use the frozen artifact without solving or simulating boards.

### Shared-board sampled payoffs

`SharedBoardMultiwayShowdownOracle` offers a reproducible sampled-payoff path for the six-seat game. For one physical six-hand deal, it draws one seeded sequence of boards, evaluates each player's hand once per board, and derives showdown shares and standard errors for all **57** active subsets of size two or more. A bounded one-deal cache means the game's repeated subset requests do not resample boards. The previous `SeededMultiwayShowdownOracle` remains unchanged for existing saved-pack reproducibility. For a six-seat deal, the new path needs one board stream instead of 57 separately seeded streams and six hand evaluations per board instead of 186 across those separate subsets. The subset shares remain sampled estimates, with per-seat standard errors; using a common board stream does not make them exact.

Tests check sharing and cache behavior, deterministic results independent of call order and range weights, agreement with exact board enumeration for heads-up, three-way, and six-way subsets within sampling error, invalid-card rejection, and construction of a two-deal six-seat game with one stream per deal. This reduces showdown preprocessing work; it does not quantify error from approximating a larger private-card distribution.

An opt-in cache can now retain up to 64 physical-deal streams and extend their board budgets without replaying earlier samples. The [payoff sampling audit](sixmax-payoff-sampling-audit.md#retained-board-streams-and-tighter-targets) uses this path and records actual board evaluations, halving most board work on a doubling ladder while preserving fresh-run estimates exactly.

### Expanded private-card chance

`SixMaxJointDealSampler` draws each seat independently from its weighted physical-combo range, rejects card collisions, and counts accepted six-hand deals. Rejection gives an empirical sample from the blocker-conditioned product distribution; it does not certify the frequencies of unsampled deals. `SixMaxPreflopCheckdownGame.fromSampledDeals` solves that finite empirical support and labels it `EMPIRICAL_JOINT_DEALS`, including the accepted-draw count. The exact range-product constructor is now allowed up to 64 legal deals under the shared public/private size cap and is labelled `EXACT_RANGE_PRODUCT`. The convergence audit and research-trainer questions carry this chance-model distinction. Their showdown-payoff standard error excludes uncertainty from an empirical chance distribution.

A [reproducible exact-versus-empirical comparison](sixmax-expanded-chance-research.md) uses the same 64-deal, 100bb shove-only fixture and the same seeded showdown boards for both chance paths. Its empirical 256-draw frequencies had total variation 0.227 from the known uniform distribution, even though all 64 deals appeared. The comparison establishes that the larger bounded game runs and keeps chance provenance visible; it does not validate a general six-max policy.

One synthetic accounting control makes the rake sensitivity concrete. With UTG shoving 100bb, four seats folding, and BB facing the final decision, give BB a 49.5% showdown share. The pot after a BB call is 200.5bb, so calling has **−0.7525bb** EV without rake versus **−1bb** for folding. A declared 5% rake capped at 1bb lowers the called pot to 199.5bb and call EV to **−1.2475bb**, reversing the preference. These shares are synthetic test inputs, not measured equity for a particular matchup. Separate controls verify physical-board exact enumeration, blockers in folded hands, range weighting, a three-way called pot, postflop rejection, and propagation of sampled-payoff error.

The bridge is checked against the independently implemented forced-shove multiway game for **all 32** possible call/fold outcomes after a UTG 100bb shove, both with and without rake, on one six-hand deal with exact board enumeration. Every per-seat payoff agrees within floating-point tolerance. Additional tests replay the documented five-bet hand, a three-way all-in, 300 seeded legal betting histories, invalid overlapping hole cards and malformed showdown shares. For the five-bet example with a synthetic 75%/25% showdown split, 5% rake capped at 1bb changes the called-pot utilities to **+50.375bb UTG** and **−49.875bb BTN**; the remaining −1.5bb is the folded blinds, and the house receives 1bb. This is an accounting control, not a claim about that hand's true equity.

This is **not a new solved six-max strategy**. The rule tree does not yet assign credible starting ranges by position or a postflop continuation value to call/check branches. Its terminal bridge assumes equal stacks; the separate forced-shove model handles unequal-stack side pots. A [research handoff](preflop-flop-transition-research.md) now carries a heads-up pot and explicitly action-conditioned ranges to a flop spot, but no validated preflop policy supplies those range likelihoods yet. The current postflop validation packs use synthetic ranges assigned at their starting street, so they cannot be plugged into these branches as credible continuation values without policy provenance, broader flop solving and error measurement.

Run `mvn -pl solver -am test` for the betting-order, pot-accounting, terminal-boundary and saved-pack regression tests. The full solver suite exercises all committed validation packs after the stricter history check.
