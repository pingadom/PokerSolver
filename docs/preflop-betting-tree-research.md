# Bounded six-seat preflop betting rules

`SixMaxPreflopBetting` is the next solver foundation after the forced-shove fixtures. It advances all six seats in UTG, HJ, CO, BTN, SB, BB order, starting from posted 0.5bb/1bb blinds. A configured menu limits raise-to amounts; the reference 100bb game offers 3, 10, 22, 40 and 100bb. The engine enforces turn order, total commitment, minimum full raises, no action after folding, and the big blind's option to check when no one raises. A raise reopens action for every other live seat. All chip amounts use integer millionths of a big blind internally.

Each immutable state exposes committed chips, live seats, pot, amount to call, legal actions and public history. It distinguishes three outcomes:

| Outcome | What the model knows |
| --- | --- |
| Everyone else folds | The remaining seat wins the pot, including folded blind money; per-seat terminal chip payoffs need no hidden cards. |
| All live seats commit 100bb | Given six distinct physical hole-card combinations, a showdown oracle can now value every surviving seat and settle the pot. |
| Two or more live seats finish below 100bb | The state is `POSTFLOP_CONTINUATION_REQUIRED`. It has no invented terminal EV. |

The existing UTG-versus-BTN five-bet spot now replays its action history through these rules at construction. It still has the same content hash, saved solution and restricted shove/fold continuation. The stronger validation rejects histories that the earlier contribution-only check could accept, such as swapping the HJ and CO actions or making a below-minimum re-raise. The reference history leaves UTG facing an 18bb call in a 63.5bb pot. If UTG shoves and BTN folds, UTG's net win is 41.5bb; if BTN calls, the final pot is 201.5bb. Those amounts match [ADR-004](decisions/ADR-004-preflop-validation-game.md).

`SixMaxPreflopTerminalPayoff` now connects those public betting states to `AllInSidePots`. It returns the utility of **each** seat, rake paid, active-seat mask and maximum payoff sampling error. A fold win can be settled from public history alone. For an all-in showdown, the caller supplies all six physical hole-card combinations in seat order, including folded hands, so removed cards cannot reappear on the board. The same oracle interface supports exact board enumeration or a declared sampled estimate. A configurable rake rule applies only when explicitly supplied; the default no-rake rule preserves earlier fixtures. A state that needs flop continuation is rejected instead of receiving a made-up showdown EV.

## Final-decision conditional EV

`SixMaxFinalDecisionEv.evaluate` values the last player's fold and call when both branches finish preflop and the call leads to an all-in showdown. It accepts the betting game and decision, the acting player's shown physical combo, six **action-conditioned** weighted ranges in UTG-to-BB order, a declared rake rule, and a showdown oracle. It enumerates legal joint deals, including cards held by players who already folded. The shown hand is fixed; opponent weights are multiplied and renormalized *after* removing card collisions. Log weights avoid overflow from large range weights. The fold payoff is the negative of chips already committed by the acting player; the call payoff averages full six-seat terminal settlements. The result gives both chip EVs, their difference, the number of legal deals, and a conservative bound from showdown-payoff sampling errors. That bound excludes uncertainty in the ranges or their action likelihoods.

The exact path is deliberately capped at **64 legal joint deals** before any showdown work. Larger ranges fail explicitly rather than silently sampling or reporting an apparently exact result. An impossible shown hand, duplicate range combos, incomplete seat ranges, an earlier decision with actions still pending, and a call that needs postflop play also fail. This is a **conditional response calculation**, not a six-player equilibrium strategy: the caller must supply defensible per-seat ranges for the observed betting history. The engine does not infer those ranges from preflop actions or certify them for trainer content.

### Connecting an existing solved response policy

`SixMaxForcedShovePolicyBridge` now supplies those action-conditioned ranges for **one narrow source game**: a 100bb UTG shove, followed by HJ, CO, BTN and SB call/fold decisions, with BB last. It reads the original physical-combo ranges and the solved call/fold frequencies in `MultiwayPreflopCallGame`. For an observed public history, each preceding player's combo weight is multiplied by that player's probability of taking the observed action at the matching public prefix. The six-seat evaluator then removes overlapping physical cards and renormalizes the legal joint deals. Folded players' cards remain blockers. The bridge reuses the source game's showdown oracle and rake rule, so it cannot silently grade one strategy against a different payoff model.

This is a constrained way to carry **policy-derived likelihoods** into the newer betting engine; it does not make the synthetic source ranges realistic. The bridge rejects changed stacks, blind commitments, seat order, dead money, missing strategy keys, impossible observed actions and histories outside this forced-shove branch. In a two-deal analytic control, an HJ call changes the relative weights of two HJ combos from 1:3 to 0.8:0.6. A fold changes them to 0.2:2.4. The corresponding BB call EVs differ, and each matches an independent conditional average of the source game's terminal utilities. Additional tests solve the source game with CFR+ and verify twelve seeded BB trainer questions against the bridge under both no-rake and capped-rake rules. Neither comparison establishes equilibrium quality beyond this restricted game.

`SixMaxForcedShoveResponderEv` now carries that comparison to **all five responding seats**. For a shown physical hand and any observed prefix of HJ-to-SB actions, it weights each legal six-hand deal by the source chance probability and the earlier players' policy likelihoods. It holds the acting player's fold or call fixed, then follows the solved policy for every later responder through `SixMaxPreflopBetting`. Every terminal branch is settled by `SixMaxPreflopTerminalPayoff` with the source game's oracle and rake rule. The returned fold/call chip EV and call payoff-sampling-error bound are conditional on the observed prefix and hand; range and policy-model error are outside that bound. At most 64 reachable physical deals are accepted per evaluation, before payoff work begins.

Cross-checks compare twelve seeded questions at **each** of the five responder seats against the existing multiway trainer, both without rake and with 5% capped rake. A separate check matches the earlier BB range-based evaluator on three public histories, and a sampled-oracle control verifies error propagation through later policy decisions. This strengthens the accounting and policy-conditioning bridge, but the strategy still comes from the restricted, synthetic forced-shove game. It does not value open/raise branches, non-all-in calls, or postflop continuation.

One synthetic accounting control makes the rake sensitivity concrete. With UTG shoving 100bb, four seats folding, and BB facing the final decision, give BB a 49.5% showdown share. The pot after a BB call is 200.5bb, so calling has **−0.7525bb** EV without rake versus **−1bb** for folding. A declared 5% rake capped at 1bb lowers the called pot to 199.5bb and call EV to **−1.2475bb**, reversing the preference. These shares are synthetic test inputs, not measured equity for a particular matchup. Separate controls verify physical-board exact enumeration, blockers in folded hands, range weighting, a three-way called pot, postflop rejection, and propagation of sampled-payoff error.

The bridge is checked against the independently implemented forced-shove multiway game for **all 32** possible call/fold outcomes after a UTG 100bb shove, both with and without rake, on one six-hand deal with exact board enumeration. Every per-seat payoff agrees within floating-point tolerance. Additional tests replay the documented five-bet hand, a three-way all-in, 300 seeded legal betting histories, invalid overlapping hole cards and malformed showdown shares. For the five-bet example with a synthetic 75%/25% showdown split, 5% rake capped at 1bb changes the called-pot utilities to **+50.375bb UTG** and **−49.875bb BTN**; the remaining −1.5bb is the folded blinds, and the house receives 1bb. This is an accounting control, not a claim about that hand's true equity.

This is **not a new solved six-max strategy**. The rule tree does not yet assign credible starting ranges by position or a postflop continuation value to call/check branches. Its terminal bridge assumes equal stacks; the separate forced-shove model handles unequal-stack side pots. A [research handoff](preflop-flop-transition-research.md) now carries a heads-up pot and explicitly action-conditioned ranges to a flop spot, but no validated preflop policy supplies those range likelihoods yet. The current postflop validation packs use synthetic ranges assigned at their starting street, so they cannot be plugged into these branches as credible continuation values without policy provenance, broader flop solving and error measurement.

Run `mvn -pl solver -am test` for the betting-order, pot-accounting, terminal-boundary and saved-pack regression tests. The full solver suite exercises all committed validation packs after the stricter history check.
