# Flop-to-river solver research

PokerLab now has a bounded **three-street heads-up game** in the offline solver. It starts from a specified flop, lets BB and BTN check or make one fixed-size bet on each street, deals public turn and river cards without replacement, and evaluates seven-card showdown hands exactly. A bet can be called or folded; there are no raises or rake. Every decision uses the acting player's exact two cards, public cards and complete public betting history. The opponent's hole cards and future public cards are absent from that information set.

The synthetic [validation fixture](../solver/src/main/java/com/pokerlab/solver/FlopTurnRiverValidationSpot.java) uses flop `2c 3d 8s`, a 20bb starting pot, 80bb remaining per player, and one 10bb bet size on each street. BB has `Ah As` or `6s 7s`; BTN has `Kc Kd` or `Qc Qd`, equally weighted. These ranges are prescribed at the flop. They are **not** derived from preflop actions and must not be described as a 6-max cash chart or a preflop call continuation.

The [game definition](../solver/src/main/java/com/pokerlab/solver/FlopTurnRiverSpot.java) includes a declared list of possible turn cards in its content hash. Its small research mode uses five candidate turn cards: `4h 5s 9c Ts Ad`. A card blocked by either private hand is omitted, then the remaining candidates are equally likely. The river is always drawn from **all 44** physically legal cards after the two hole-card pairs, flop and turn. This is a coherent restricted-deck game, but its turn chance is an abstraction of Hold'em. `withFullTurnDeck()` switches to all 49 non-flop cards, of which exactly 45 remain legal for each joint private deal. That mode uses exact public-card chance on both streets. The solver accepts either mode; it never treats a sampled turn as a physical full-deck outcome.

The starting pot is split conceptually so the game remains zero-sum. A fold on the flop pays ±10bb, a fold after one called flop bet pays ±20bb, and a fold after called flop and turn bets pays ±30bb. At showdown, called bets add 10bb per street to the winner's centered utility; three called bets give ±40bb. This convention accounts for committed chips but does not include rake. The max of all three bet sizes must fit inside the specified remaining stack.

The solver uses alternating full-tree CFR+ and [information-set best responses](../solver/src/main/java/com/pokerlab/solver/HeadsUpBestResponse.java). The latter measure exploitability **within the exact game passed to them**. In particular, a tiny best-response gap for the five-card turn game would not bound error against full-deck Hold'em. Tests check blockers and chance probabilities at both public deals, private-card separation in information sets, chip payoffs on each street, stable game identity, and improving best-response bounds.

The [chance audit](../solver/src/main/java/com/pokerlab/solver/FlopTurnChanceAudit.java) exhaustively evaluates every legal turn–river runout for each of the four joint private deals under a forced check-down policy. It compares that full-deck result with check-down under the restricted turn list. For this fixture the exact weighted check-down value is **+0.202020bb for BB**, versus **+1.409091bb** in the five-card game: a **+1.207071bb bias**. The largest per-deal absolute difference is **1.464646bb**. In full-deck mode the difference is zero. This is an informative abstraction diagnostic for one fixed policy, **not a bound on strategic abstraction error**. It is large enough that the restricted solution should not be packaged as training content.

After `mvn -q -pl solver -am test-compile`, reproduce the measurements from the repository root with:

```powershell
java -Xmx3g -cp 'solver/target/classes;engine/target/classes' com.pokerlab.solver.BenchmarkFlopTurnRiver 100
java -Xmx3g -cp 'solver/target/classes;engine/target/classes' com.pokerlab.solver.BenchmarkFlopTurnRiver 100 --full-turn-deck
```

At 100 iterations, the restricted game has **16,688 information sets** and a **0.194926bb** exact-game best-response gap. The full-deck game has **156,224 information sets** and a **0.198039bb** gap. At 500 iterations the full-deck gap falls to **0.024049bb**. On the development machine, full-deck solving took **43.7 seconds** for 100 iterations and **243.2 seconds** for 500, compared with **4.7 seconds** for 100 iterations of the five-card game. This roughly ninefold game-size increase is the next scale problem. Timing depends on hardware and JVM warm-up; the gap and information-set counts are the reproducible comparison. A trial cache of complete chance outcomes was slower (48.4 seconds for 100 full-deck iterations) and was removed. Neither strategy is a reviewed trainer solution.

No flop trainer API or saved solution pack is enabled from this research game. The next backend step is to solve the **full-deck** fixture to a useful measured gap within a practical offline budget, then add a validated immutable pack. Only after that should a flop decision or connected flop partial hand enter the research trainer. A future preflop-to-flop transition also needs preceding-street ranges and actions, realistic bet sizes and rake; this synthetic flop fixture cannot supply them by itself.
